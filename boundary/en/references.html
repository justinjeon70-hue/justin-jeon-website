Classical Sources

  • Aeschylus, the Oresteia trilogy (Agamemnon, The Libation Bearers, The Eumenides), 458 BCE. The Greek text and Herbert Weir Smyth’s English translation follow the Perseus Digital Library. The passages cited are Agamemnon 176–183 (pathei mathos, χάρις βίαιος, σωφρονεῖν) and 248–250, together with the Areopagus trial in The Eumenides and the closing transformation of the Furies into the Kindly Ones. Agamemnon 179 differs by edition between ἔν θ᾽ ὕπνῳ (Smyth) and ἀνθ᾽ ὕπνου (the current Loeb).
  • Nagarjuna, Mulamadhyamakakarika. The sections on dependent origination and the absence of intrinsic nature.
  • Sophocles, Philoctetes, 409 BCE. The archer abandoned on Lemnos for a wound that would not heal, and Neoptolemus, who goes to deceive him and gives the bow back.
  • I Ching. Hexagram Qian, the “Wenyan” commentary and the top line kanglong youhui; Hexagram Fu (Earth over Thunder), the judgment and the “Tuan” commentary (fu qi jian tiandi zhi xin), the six line statements (buyuan fu, xiu fu, pin fu, du fu, dun fu, mi fu) and the “Xiang” commentary on the second line (xiu fu zhi ji, yi xia ren ye); the “Xici” commentary, yi yin yi yang zhi wei dao and qiong ze bian, bian ze tong, tong ze jiu.

Other Sources

  • National Institute of Ecology, survey of biodiversity in the DMZ area. 6,168 species were confirmed across eight zones, and 102 of the Korean peninsula’s 267 endangered species were recorded in this area.
  • Joo-Hwan Kim’s discussion of the boundary. He defines meditation as standing at the boundary and beholding the boundary, moving by way of the Markov blanket and Varela’s enactivism to Nagarjuna’s Madhyamaka. This is the source of the sentence borrowed in the prologue. It parts from this book in concluding that the boundary is ultimately empty. The same argument appears in the lectures on his own channel, “Standing at the Boundary and Noticing the Boundary — The Markov Blanket Model and Enactivism” (March 30, 2025) and “True Love Is Beholding the Emptiness of the Boundary” (May 11, 2025).
  • World Health Organization, Preamble to the Constitution of the WHO, 1948. The definition of health.
  • Escher, M. C., “Day and Night,” woodcut, 1938.
  • The film As One, directed by Moon Hyun-sung, 2012.
  • The 41st World Table Tennis Championships, Chiba, Japan, April 24 – May 6, 1991. The unified Korean team.
  • The 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics and Paralympics.
  • NPR, “In Korean DMZ, wildlife thrives. Some conservationists worry peace could disrupt it,” April 20, 2019. Remarks by Kim Seung-ho, director of the DMZ Ecology Research Institute, and the plant researcher Jeong Su-young.
  • Bless derives from Old English blētsian and shares a root with blood. The root of happiness is hap, meaning chance.

Not Yet Verified

  • The Ham Min-bok quotations in the prologue and epilogue are taken from “Flower,” the title poem of the collection On Every Boundary, Flowers Bloom. The prologue quotes the opening (the first line and the first two lines of the second stanza); the epilogue quotes the final stanza. As this is contemporary poetry, permission from the poet and the publisher is required before publication.
  • The sentence the prologue credits to Joo-Hwan Kim is one he has repeated on many occasions, and it has not been settled which occasion to cite as the source. The same argument appears in the lectures on his own channel (March 30 and May 11, 2025), and a separate lecture series and panel appearances by him exist on Choi Gyeom’s YouTube channel. The author should confirm where he first heard the phrase and fix the citation.
  • J. G. Sparks (2017), which appeared in the notes to Chapter Nine, has been removed from this list because it could not be determined what work it refers to.
  • The story that the koi carp grows small in a tank and large in a river is widely circulated, but no scholarly basis for it could be confirmed. This is stated in the text as well.
  • Quoting contemporary poetry in the text requires permission from the poet and the publisher. Confirmation is also needed before publication from those who appear under their real names.

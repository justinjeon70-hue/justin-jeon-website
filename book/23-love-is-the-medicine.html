Part Five: Oxytocin for a Healthier You

Chapter 23: Love Is the Medicine

-- The Oxytocin Effect

The American sociologist Ira Reiss once described the four stages of love as a turning wheel. Though the theory dates back a few decades, his Wheel Theory of Love was one of the first relational development models to conceptualize the entire process by which two people meet, court, deepen their relationship, and ultimately choose each other as partners. According to Reiss, as a couple moves toward greater intimacy, they pass through a cyclical and sequential process with four interlocking stages — like the spokes of a wheel: rapport, self-revelation, mutual dependency, and personality need fulfillment. First, the couple discovers shared experiences and similarities, building rapport. Then, seeking a more satisfying relationship, they move into the stage of self-revelation — opening up to each other. As they come to know each other deeply, they grow increasingly dependent on one another, until they reach the stage where they fulfill each other's needs and desires. Reiss believed that every love relationship follows this path.

Looking back at my own life, I can see that I traveled along this very wheel of love — meeting my wife as a complete stranger, falling in love, and eventually making it to the altar. It started with curiosity, then love pulled me in, and now we've become indispensable to each other. "For the one carelessly discarded like me, the only one who cried for me — I want to lean on those broad shoulders. Don't wake me from that dream." These are lyrics from "All I Know Is Love," a signature song by the beloved Korean singer Sim Su-bong. In an interview, she once revealed that she grew up without a father and had never been loved by a man — her husband was the first man to ever show her love. Human beings stake everything on love, make great sacrifices for love. Love is our greatest vulnerability and, at the same time, our greatest strength. Love is the one ability God placed within us. That's why Rumi, the great Sufi master, said, "Love is the symbol of God's great beauty."

Love, That Mighty Force

In 1942, Karl A. Menninger wrote in his book Love Against Hate: "Science is merely our servant. We use it to make guns, and we use it to make fever reducers. We use it to build bridges, and we use it to build bombs." Menninger went on to say that when a psychologist speaks about science, it's like "a voice crying in the wilderness." He argued that because so many of the world's diseases originate in the mind, psychology and psychoanalysis are about healing the mind that causes those diseases. And the medicine that heals the mind, he declared, is love.

In his book, he quoted Freud, who reportedly said near the end of his life: "The idea that love can cure hatred is an entirely new perspective. And it seems no one has yet dared to actually try it."[22-1]

When Menninger wrote this book, World War II was raging, and nations were competing to build ever more effective weapons for killing on a massive scale. Even considering the harshness of the times, his insight — that human disease can arise from personality, thought patterns, and daily attitudes — is both fascinating and almost mystical. He went further still, essentially making a claim that sounds like something from scripture — "Love heals all sorrow" — but framing it in the language of science. Could the greed and personality flaws of national leaders, their insecurities and fragile self-esteem, be what drive wars between nations? Such wars have claimed countless lives. Someone lost a son, someone a husband, someone a father, someone a mother. Innumerable people lost parts of their bodies and live the rest of their lives with disabilities. Even more live with the lifelong trauma that war inflicts. Menninger prescribed love as the cure for all of it. Could it be that Menninger already knew about the power of oxytocin before anyone else?

Love Through the Lens of Oxytocin

Of course, Menninger was talking about how love can heal individual psychological problems and even mental illness. And indeed, oxytocin heals PTSD, repairs the damage caused by stress-related diseases, resolves the psychological problems born of loneliness, makes us more likable and optimistic, and mends broken relationships by rebuilding trust. When you consider that stress actually causes stomach ulcers, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes — and can even trigger cancer — oxytocin starts to look like something close to a universal cure.

In South Korea, suicide is the number one cause of death for people in their teens through thirties. When you account for the fact that oxytocin could potentially address all of the country's top five causes of death, the implications are staggering. Jesus said, "Love your neighbor as yourself." To love our neighbors, we must first love our own bodies. The fact that people who attempt suicide have significantly lower oxytocin levels than those who don't — what does this tell us about South Korea, a country that leads the world in suicide rates?[22-2]

As a researcher who has spent his entire career in this field, I have devoted my life to proving a deceptively simple concept: exercise is medicine. I've spent years studying how to help more people become healthier and happier through physical activity. Along the way, I encountered oxytocin — and the more I learned about it, the more I studied its effects on health, the more I found myself drawn past the motto of "exercise is medicine" toward a new, even more fundamental truth: love is medicine.

Love. Love with everything you have. And you will live.
